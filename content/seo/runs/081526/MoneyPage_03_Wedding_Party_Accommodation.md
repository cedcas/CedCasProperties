# Where Your Wedding Party Stays in Lipa

Every wedding in Lipa has the same gap in the plan.

You've booked the venue. It seats eighty, it photographs beautifully, and it sleeps nobody. So the entourage scatters across hotel rooms, the bridesmaids get ready in three different places, and somebody spends the morning of the wedding driving between them.

We rent whole houses in Lipa City — up to fifteen people under one roof, minutes from where you're getting married.

## The problem nobody solves for you

Search for wedding accommodation in Lipa and you'll get hotels or you'll get venues. The guides that rank for wedding venues mention accommodation in a single line — *"proximity to a variety of hotels ensures ample options"* — and name none of them.

That's not an answer. Here's what it leaves you managing:

**Eight rooms, eight keys, eight breakfasts, nobody together.** Hotels price per room, so a party of twelve becomes six or seven bookings. Everyone checks in separately, eats separately, and the group you specifically wanted around you is down a corridor.

**No kitchen, no space, no morning-of.** Hair and makeup for six people needs counter space and mirrors and somewhere to put a garment bag. A hotel room has a bathroom and a bed. The photographer needs light and floor space; they'll spend the morning shooting around a headboard.

**Checkout at noon on the day after.** The reception ends late. Everyone's out by twelve the next morning, which is exactly when nobody wants to be packing.

## What a whole house changes

**Everyone under one roof the night before.** The part people actually remember — the whole group in one place the evening before, without a lobby or a room-service menu.

**A real kitchen for the morning of.** Counter space for makeup, a proper table for breakfast that isn't a tray, a fridge for whatever needs to stay cold. Somebody's aunt will cook. This is a feature.

**Room for the photographer to work.** Getting-ready shots need light and space. A living room gives both; a hotel room gives a window and a bed.

**One booking, one payment, one set of house rules.** Not seven reservations under seven names.

## Two houses, five doors apart

Here is the part that makes this work for a wedding, and it's worth being precise about.

We have **two houses in the same village — five doors apart, about a two-minute walk.** Between them they sleep **up to 24 people**.

### The big house — up to 15
Three bedrooms across two floors. The entourage, or both families the night before. Book it as the configuration that matches your headcount — up to 7, up to 11, or the full 15.

### The second house — up to 9
A two-minute walk away. Book it for the couple, the parents, or the overflow that doesn't fit in the big house. Configured as either a 1BR (up to 5) or the full 2BR (up to 9).

**Why two houses beats one big one:** the bridal party can take over the big house for hair, makeup and the photographer, while the parents and early sleepers have their own roof — and nobody is driving between them. Everyone is still together for breakfast.

> ⚠️ **One honest constraint.** Each house takes **one booking at a time** — the different guest counts you'll see are configurations of the same house, not separate units. So the real question isn't "how many listings can I book," it's "do two houses sleeping up to 24 between them cover my party?" Message us with your headcount and we'll tell you straight whether we fit.

> 📌 **Copy rule for whoever edits this page:** always write **"sleeps up to 24 people,"** never "24 beds." The sleeping capacity comes from a mix — a queen sleeps two, a daybed sleeps three — so bed count and guest count are different numbers, and quoting beds would understate the house and invite a "but there are only N beds" complaint on arrival.

## Booking for a wedding

**Book early.** Wedding dates cluster hard — December through February, and every long weekend. The Full Family House is the first to go.

**Holding dates.** Message us before you book if the venue date isn't locked. We'd rather hold a conversation than take a booking you have to move.

**Payment.** GCash, BPI InstaPay (no fees), or credit card via Stripe (6% processing). Full cancellation terms on the [FAQ page](/faq).

**Gatherings are fine — tell us first.** A get-together at the house is permitted under our house rules as long as you let us know in advance. That covers the night-before dinner, the morning-of preparations, and family dropping by. What we can't accommodate is an unannounced event, or using the house as the reception venue itself — we're where your people stay, not where the wedding happens.

## Questions from couples

**Can we hold the ceremony or reception there?**
No. We're accommodation, not a venue — no ceremony space, no reception capacity, no catering. Book your venue, then book us for where everyone sleeps.

**Can the bridal party get ready at the house?**
Yes, and it's one of the main reasons people book us. Kitchen and living space for hair and makeup, and room for a photographer to work. Just tell us in advance.

**Can we have a dinner the night before?**
Yes — gatherings are permitted with the host informed. Let us know numbers and timing when you book.

**How many people can you actually sleep?**
Up to 15 in the big house and up to 9 in the second — **24 in total**, across two houses a two-minute walk apart.

**How far ahead should we book?**
For a wedding date, as early as you have it. Peak season books out months ahead.

**Can we book both houses?**
Yes — that's the usual arrangement for a wedding party, and they're five doors apart. What you can't do is book two configurations of the *same* house: the different guest counts are options on one property, not separate units. Tell us your headcount and we'll work out the split.

**What if the wedding date moves?**
Talk to us early. Cancellation terms are on the [FAQ page](/faq), but a conversation usually beats a policy.
