# Fact Checking Guide — HavenInLipa Blog

**Owner:** Content team / blog editor (verification) + Cassandra (pre-publish flag)
**Status:** First version — 2026-05-24, after the June–August 2026 article batch (#15–26 + R1) was drafted
**Goal:** Catch factual errors before publish — historical dates, business details, prices, religious claims, holiday calendars, and verifiable specs — so HIL never publishes something a knowledgeable Lipa local would correct in the comments.

---

## Why this matters now

The HavenInLipa voice is built on being honest and locally rooted. The credibility comes from naming real places (Casa Marikit, Beegee's, Café de Lipa), using real PHP budgets, and treating Lipa as a real city, not a tourist abstraction. The flip side of that voice is that **any factual error damages the whole brand more than it would damage a generic travel blog**. A wrong price at Beegee's is forgivable. A wrong year for Lipa's cityhood charter is not.

The June–August 2026 batch pushed deeper into history (#17 coffee, #21 Casa de Segunda, #22 Carmel, #24 Charter Day) and religion (#22 Carmel of Lipa), where the cost of an error is higher than in the earlier "weekend itinerary" pieces. This guide locks down a workflow for verifying the high-risk claims before each Monday publish.

It's also a record so we don't re-verify the same facts every quarter when the next batch goes out.

---

## Five fact-check principles

1. **Verify the verifiable.** Dates, RA numbers, official titles, addresses, opening hours, prices, geographic facts — these have a single right answer. Check them.
2. **Frame the unverifiable carefully.** Historical narratives, religious events, contested claims — write them so the framing is accurate even if specific details shift. "Was reported," "is associated with," "traditionally celebrated" — these are honest hedges, not weasel words.
3. **Single source ≠ verified.** If only one Facebook post says Casa de Segunda is open Tuesdays, it's "probably" open Tuesdays. Cross-check before quoting it as fact.
4. **Date stamp ranges.** Prices, hours, fees — always written with "as of [year]" or "verify current rate." Never quote a price as if it's a forever fact.
5. **When in doubt, hedge and flag.** A vague-but-correct line ("entrance is modest, around ₱50–₱150") is better than a precise-but-wrong line ("entrance is ₱75"). When you can't verify, write it loosely and add a "Cassandra — please verify before publishing" comment in the blogger guide.

---

## The risk matrix

Not every fact is equally risky. Rank them:

| Risk level | Fact type | Cost if wrong | Action |
|---|---|---|---|
| 🔴 **High** | Historical dates, RA numbers, official titles, religious-event details, named people | Reputational — locals correct us in comments; outreach contacts lose trust | Verify with 2+ authoritative sources before publish |
| 🟠 **Medium** | Business hours, holiday-day-of-week, official addresses, government dates | Operational — readers show up to a closed door | Verify with one current authoritative source (FB page, GMaps, official site) within 7 days of publish |
| 🟡 **Low** | Prices, ranges, "popular" claims, generic descriptions | Cosmetic — readers shrug | Use ranges with "as of writing" framing; verify if convenient, hedge if not |
| 🟢 **Negligible** | Voice, opinion, recommendations, framing | None | No verification needed |

The vast majority of HIL content is 🟢 and 🟡. The places to spend verification time are 🔴 and 🟠.

## Verification source tiers

When fact-checking, use the highest-tier source available:

| Tier | Source | When to use |
|---|---|---|
| **Tier 1** (most authoritative) | Government records (RA text, Official Gazette, PSA, City Government of Lipa, Archdiocese of Lipa) | Historical dates, RA numbers, holiday calendars, official boundaries, charter status |
| **Tier 1** | Academic/institutional publications (Philippine Coffee Board, SEARCA, university archives, museum publications) | Coffee history, agricultural facts, heritage documentation |
| **Tier 2** | Verified institutional social media + official websites (Casa de Segunda FB page, Café de Lipa website, Mary Mediatrix Medical Center site, San Sebastian Cathedral parish page) | Operating hours, current programs, official prices |
| **Tier 2** | Major news outlets (PDI, Rappler, GMA News, ABS-CBN) for recent events | Religious-event status updates, contemporary news |
| **Tier 3** (cross-check, never trust alone) | TripAdvisor, Google Maps, travel blogs, Wikipedia | Cross-reference only — don't quote as primary source |
| **Tier 4** (avoid) | Random social media posts, unsourced claim aggregators, AI-generated content | Never cite |

**Rule:** A 🔴 high-risk claim needs at least one Tier 1 source confirming it. A 🟠 medium-risk claim needs at least one Tier 2 source. 🟡 low-risk claims can ride on Tier 3 with hedged framing.

## Article-by-article fact-check examples (#15–25)

These are the obvious sanity checks per article. They aren't exhaustive — they're the **2 to 4 highest-risk claims per article** that Cassandra (or whoever publishes) should verify in the week before the Monday publish.

### Article #15 — Independence Day Long Weekend (publish Jun 8, 2026)

🔴 **Independence Day 2026 falls on Friday, June 12.**
- Verify: Pull up a 2026 calendar. June 12, 2026 is a Friday. ✅
- Source: Any standard 2026 calendar; Official Gazette of the Philippines for the year's national holiday declaration.

🟠 **Lipa City Hall hosts a flag-raising ceremony at Plaza Independencia, typically 7:00–7:30 AM on June 12.**
- Verify: The City Government of Lipa Facebook page typically posts the schedule the week before. Confirm exact time/location before publish. If no confirmation by Jun 5, soften the line to "City Hall typically holds a flag-raising ceremony on Independence Day morning — check the City Government of Lipa social media for the year's schedule."
- Source: facebook.com/CityGovernmentofLipa (Tier 2).

🟡 **"Casa Marikit is TripAdvisor's #1 Lipa restaurant."**
- Verify: Quick check on TripAdvisor — is Casa Marikit still ranked #1 for Lipa City? Rankings shift quarterly.
- Source: TripAdvisor "Restaurants in Lipa City" page (Tier 3 — cross-check).
- If they've dropped to #2 or #3, change to "one of Lipa's most-loved restaurants" — same effect, no precision claim that can be wrong.

### Article #16 — What to Do in Lipa When It Rains (publish Jun 15, 2026)

🟠 **"Mary Mediatrix Medical Center and Lipa Medix Medical Center both have 24/7 emergency services."**
- This claim is reused from earlier articles (#11). Verify both hospitals still operate, names are correct, and 24/7 ER status holds.
- Source: Each hospital's official site or FB page (Tier 2).

🟡 **"Casa de Segunda entrance fee ₱50 to ₱100."**
- Verify via Casa de Segunda's FB page or a call to the museum. Fees occasionally adjust.
- The article uses a range with "may vary — confirm at the entrance" hedge, which protects against minor drift. If the fee is now meaningfully higher (e.g., ₱200), update.

🟢 **Rainy-season weather pattern claims** ("rain peaks July–August," "afternoon showers 1–3 hours," "temperature drops to 22–26°C").
- These are general PAGASA-consistent climate facts. No need to verify individual claims.

### Article #17 — Lipa Barako Coffee Heritage (publish Jun 22, 2026)

🔴 **"Coffee leaf rust epidemic destroys coffee crops in Brazil, Ceylon (Sri Lanka), and Java in the 1860s–1870s."**
- Verify the regional sequence and rough dates. Brazil was actually affected later than Ceylon — Ceylon's rust crisis (Hemileia vastatrix) hit in the 1860s–1880s and destroyed its coffee industry; Brazil's was different and partial. The Java disruption is correct.
- Source: Academic agricultural histories, FAO documentation (Tier 1).
- If the chronology is slightly off, adjust to: "A devastating coffee leaf rust epidemic struck Ceylon, Java, and other major coffee regions in the 1860s–1880s, disrupting global supply." This is more defensible.

🔴 **"Spanish colonial government elevates Lipa to a villa (chartered town) in 1887."**
- Verify: The full title was *La Villa de Lipa*, granted via Royal Decree. The exact year is sometimes cited as 1887, sometimes 1888 depending on source (decree date vs. effective date).
- Source: Lipa City Government history records, Batangas historical society publications (Tier 1).
- If 1887 is contested, hedge to "in the late 1880s."

🔴 **"1889 — coffee leaf rust finally reaches the Philippines."**
- Verify the year. Some sources cite 1889, others cite the early 1890s for when rust reached Philippine plantations at scale.
- Source: SEARCA, Philippine Coffee Board, agricultural archives (Tier 1).
- If contested, hedge to "by the late 1880s / early 1890s."

🟡 **"Coffea liberica is one of the four main coffee species commercially grown in the world."**
- Verify: The four standard commercial species are Arabica (*C. arabica*), Robusta (*C. canephora*), Liberica (*C. liberica*), and Excelsa (*C. excelsa* — sometimes classified as a subspecies of Liberica).
- Source: ICO, agricultural references (Tier 1).
- ✅ The article's framing is correct.

🟡 **"Barako = stud or wild boar in Tagalog."**
- Both translations are standard. ✅ No change needed.

### Article #18 — Best Lomi in Lipa (publish Jun 29, 2026)

🟠 **Pricing claims** ("Regular lomi ₱70–₱120," "Special ₱150–₱250," "Beegee's regular ₱120, special ₱250," "Lomi King regular ₱90, special ₱220").
- These are quoted with reasonable ranges, but prices in 2026 may have moved. Cassandra should call or visit Beegee's and Lomi King once in the week before publish and update if prices have shifted >₱30 from the stated ranges.
- Source: Direct call or visit (Tier 2). Beegee's FB page sometimes posts a menu (Tier 2).

🟡 **"Filipino lomi is descended from Chinese-Filipino noodle dishes that arrived through the trade between Fujian and the Philippines centuries ago."**
- The Hokkien *lor mee* origin is the standard accepted history. ✅ Framing is accurate.

🟢 **"Lipa is the lomi capital of the Philippines."**
- This is a widely-accepted local-pride claim. Not contested. ✅

### Article #19 — Work From Lipa Rainy Season (publish Jul 6, 2026)

🟠 **"HavenInLipa runs fiber at both properties — measured at around 400 Mbps at the Cozy 1BR."**
- Verify: Run an actual speed test at both units the week before publish. Update the number if it's meaningfully different (e.g., if it now runs at 300 or 500 Mbps).
- Source: Speedtest.net or fast.com on-site (Tier 1 — direct measurement).

🟠 **Banking claims** ("BPI, BDO, Metrobank, Security Bank within a 10-minute drive").
- Verify: All four chains still have a Lipa branch within 10 minutes of HavenInLipa. Banks occasionally close branches.
- Source: Google Maps + bank locator pages (Tier 2).

🟡 **"A 14-night booking at the 1BR works out to about ₱28,000 weekly rate."**
- This is HIL's own pricing — verify the current weekly rate before publish. The article uses round numbers as illustrative; if the actual rate is meaningfully different (e.g., ₱32,000), update.

🟢 **General productivity/remote-work framing.**
- Opinion, voice. No verification needed.

### Article #20 — Lipa vs. Tagaytay Comparison (publish Jul 13, 2026)

🟠 **Elevation claims** ("Lipa at ~300 m, Tagaytay at ~600 m").
- Verify: Lipa City elevation is ~300m (city center) but varies by location; Tagaytay City is roughly 600m at the rim.
- Source: Philippine Atmospheric, Geophysical and Astronomical Services Administration (PAGASA), topographic maps (Tier 1).
- ✅ The claims are approximately correct; the "~" prefix protects against exact-elevation pedantry.

🟠 **Drive time claims** ("Manila → Lipa 60–90 min off-peak; Manila → Tagaytay 90–180 min off-peak").
- Verify: These are reasonable real-world estimates but vary by exact origin and time of day. The current text uses ranges, which protects against single-data-point disputes.
- ✅ Acceptable as-is.

🟡 **Rental cost comparison** ("Tagaytay rental ₱4,500–8,000/night vs Lipa ₱2,500–4,000/night for comparable quality").
- These are illustrative ranges. The exact spread varies by listing. Use as written.

🟢 **Decision matrix, "what each destination doesn't have"** — opinion/positioning content. ✅

### Article #21 — Casa de Segunda Heritage Walk (publish Jul 20, 2026)

🔴 **"Segunda Katigbak (1860–1944)" + "He met her in Manila around 1877."**
- Verify the birth/death years and the meeting date. Standard sources: Rizal's own memoirs, NHCP biographical records.
- Source: National Historical Commission of the Philippines (NHCP), Rizal Park / Rizaliana archives (Tier 1).
- Standard biographical references cite Segunda Katigbak's lifespan and the Rizal connection consistently — most sources agree she was 14 when she met Rizal in 1877. Confirm the years if any source disagrees.

🟠 **Casa de Segunda hours and entrance fee.**
- The article hedges: "typically 9 AM to 5 PM, closed Mondays" and "₱50 to ₱150" — both with "confirm" caveats.
- Verify with a call or FB message the week before publish. If the museum is currently closed (renovations, temporary closure), this article shouldn't publish on schedule — flag to Cedric.
- Source: Casa de Segunda FB page or direct call (Tier 2).

🟡 **"Jose Rizal's first love" framing.**
- This is the standard historical positioning. Rizal himself wrote about Segunda Katigbak as a significant early attachment, sometimes characterized as his first love. ✅ Accurate framing.

🟢 **"The most underrated stop in Lipa."**
- Opinion/voice. ✅

### Article #22 — Carmel of Lipa & Cathedral (publish Jul 27, 2026)

🔴 **HIGHEST-RISK ARTICLE.** Every claim about the 1948 Marian events needs careful framing.

🔴 **"In 1948, a young Carmelite postulant, Sister Teresita Castillo, reported a series of Marian apparitions and unusual phenomena."**
- Verify the name (Teresita Castillo), the year (1948), and the status (postulant at the time of the events).
- Source: Carmelite Order publications, Archdiocese of Lipa records, established Catholic Church historical archives (Tier 1).
- ✅ The article's framing "reported," "associated with," and "is associated with the 1948 events" is the right level of hedging for a contested apparition.

🔴 **"The institutional Catholic Church's position on the 1948 events has shifted over the decades. The events are documented; the Church's formal declarations on their supernatural character have been the subject of ongoing review."**
- This framing is accurate as of late-2020s public understanding. The Church's positions have shifted multiple times historically. **Do not write the article in a way that asserts either "officially approved" or "officially rejected" without checking the most current Vatican / Archdiocese of Lipa statement.**
- Source: Recent Archdiocese of Lipa or CBCP statements (Tier 1); reputable Catholic news outlets like Catholic News Agency, UCA News (Tier 2).
- If the formal Church position has been clarified more definitively since this article was drafted, update the framing to reflect the most current position.

🔴 **"Feast of Our Lady, Mediatrix of All Grace, traditionally observed September 12."**
- Verify the date. Sept 12 is the commonly observed feast day, but check the current liturgical calendar.
- Source: Archdiocese of Lipa liturgical calendar (Tier 1).

🟠 **"Archdiocese of Lipa" + "San Sebastian Cathedral is the principal church of the Archdiocese of Lipa."**
- Verify: Lipa is an Archdiocese (established as such; was a diocese before). Cathedral patron is Saint Sebastian.
- Source: CBCP / Archdiocese of Lipa official site (Tier 1).
- ✅ Both are correct.

🟡 **Visit etiquette and practical claims** (modest dress, photography rules, candle donations).
- Standard Catholic-site etiquette. Generic — no verification needed.

**Pre-publish review checkpoint:** Article #22 should be re-read by Cedric or Melody before the Jul 27 publish specifically for tonal sensitivity. The "tone note" in the blogger guide flags this. Don't let it auto-publish without that review.

### Article #23 — Lipa August Long-Weekends 2026 (publish Aug 3, 2026)

🔴 **Holiday dates.**
- "Friday Aug 21 → Sunday Aug 23 — Ninoy Aquino Day (Friday holiday)" — verify Aug 21, 2026 is a Friday and is the observed date for Ninoy Aquino Day. (Per Philippine law, Ninoy Aquino Day is Aug 21 — a fixed-date holiday.)
- "Friday Aug 28 → Monday Aug 31 — National Heroes Day (Monday holiday)" — verify Aug 31, 2026 is the last Monday of August. (National Heroes Day is the last Monday of August by law.)

- ✅ Both date claims are correct for 2026: Aug 21, 2026 = Friday; Aug 31, 2026 = Monday (last Monday of August).

🟠 **What's open/closed on holiday Mondays** — Casa de Segunda may close, hospitals 24/7, government offices closed, malls normal.
- General Philippine holiday operating patterns. ✅ Standard claims.

🟡 **Budget figures** (₱16,700 for 3 nights couples, ₱25,900 for 4 nights couples, etc.).
- Illustrative. Use as-written.

### Article #24 — Lipa Charter Day (publish Aug 10, 2026)

🔴 **"On August 20, 1947, President Manuel Roxas signed Republic Act No. 162, formally elevating Lipa to a chartered city."**
- Verify the date (Aug 20, 1947), the president (Manuel Roxas), and the RA number (162).
- Source: Official Gazette of the Philippines, RA 162 full text, Lipa City Government records (Tier 1).
- ✅ Standard historical record: RA 162 was approved on June 20, 1947 (signing date may vary in sources — verify exact "elevated to city" date vs. signing date). The Aug 20 date is widely cited as Lipa's Charter Day anniversary.
- **Important:** Confirm whether Aug 20 is the signing date or the effective date or the date of inauguration as a city. These can differ. The article should accurately reflect which milestone Aug 20 commemorates.

🔴 **"In 1605, the town was officially established as a Spanish-era pueblo."**
- Verify the founding year. Some sources cite 1605, others cite earlier or later dates. The exact founding date of Lipa as a pueblo varies depending on which historical milestone is being referenced (initial settlement vs. formal Spanish administrative recognition).
- Source: Lipa City Government, Batangas Provincial Historical records (Tier 1).
- If the year is contested, hedge to "in the early 1600s."

🔴 **"The Battle of Lipa in early 1945 caused significant civilian casualties and destroyed much of the urban fabric."**
- Verify. The Battle of Lipa is a documented WWII engagement. Standard sources confirm extensive destruction.
- Source: Philippine WWII archives, military history references (Tier 1).
- ✅ Generally accurate framing.

🟠 **"Spanish colonial government granted Lipa the status of villa in 1887."**
- Same claim as Article #17. Verify once, apply across both articles.

🟢 **Civic identity and "town that's also a city" framing.**
- Opinion. ✅

## Fact-check workflow per article

For each Monday publish, the verification window opens **7 days before** publish:

### 7 days before publish (Cassandra or Cedric)
- [ ] Open the article markdown file
- [ ] Open this Fact Checking Guide to the article's section
- [ ] Verify all 🔴 high-risk claims using Tier 1 sources
- [ ] Verify all 🟠 medium-risk claims using Tier 2 sources
- [ ] Update the article text if any claim has changed
- [ ] If a verification can't be confirmed, soften the language (use the hedging examples in this guide)
- [ ] Mark the article as "Fact-checked YYYY-MM-DD" in a comment at the top

### 3 days before publish
- [ ] Spot-check operating-hours / prices for any business referenced in the article (visit the FB page, do a quick Google)
- [ ] If a referenced business is closed permanently or temporarily, either substitute or remove the reference

### Day of publish (Cassandra)
- [ ] Pre-publish checklist in the blogger guide (already in each article)
- [ ] If something is uncertain at publish time, post anyway with the hedged language — don't delay the cadence

### After publish
- [ ] If a reader comments with a correction, update the article within 48 hours
- [ ] Log the correction in this guide so we know what to verify next time

## Special cases

### Religious / sensitive content (Article #22 — Carmel of Lipa)

**This article requires a different workflow** because the cost of a tonal or factual error is higher than for other pieces:

1. **Pre-publish review by Cedric AND Melody** (not just one of them) is mandatory for Article #22.
2. **Re-verify the current Church position** within 7 days of publish. The Vatican / Archdiocese of Lipa's framing of the 1948 events has shifted over decades; if there's a recent update we missed, the article needs revision.
3. **Source any direct claims about specific apparition messages or phenomena** to documented Carmelite or Archdiocesan sources only. Don't rely on devotional sites or third-party Catholic blogs.
4. **If a reader correction comes in** on this article specifically, treat it as urgent — update within 24 hours, not 48.

### Historical content (Articles #17, #21, #24)

These three articles carry the most historical claims. A few cross-article rules:

1. **Use consistent dates across articles.** If #17 says "1887 villa status" and #24 says "1888," that's a credibility-killer. Pick one verified date and apply across all three.
2. **The 1889 coffee rust date appears in both #17 and #24.** Verify once, apply to both.
3. **Lipa's 1605 founding** appears in #24. If you can't pin this to a Tier 1 source confidently, hedge to "in the early 1600s" in both articles.

### Holiday calendar content (Articles #15, #23, #25, #26)

The 2026 Philippine holiday calendar is the spine of these four articles. **One spreadsheet of verified 2026 dates** prevents errors across all four:

| Holiday | 2026 Date | Day of week | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| Independence Day | June 12 | Friday | Official Gazette ✅ |
| Ninoy Aquino Day | August 21 | Friday | Fixed-date holiday ✅ |
| Lipa Charter Day | August 20 | Thursday | Civic anniversary (not a national holiday) |
| National Heroes Day | August 31 | Monday | Last Monday of August ✅ |

**Build this table once. Reference it across all four articles. Verify it once a year for the next year's batch.**

## What NOT to fact-check

To keep the workflow sustainable, **don't** spend verification time on:

- **Voice, tone, opinion** — "Lipa is a place to be, not a place to do things" doesn't need a source
- **Recommendations** — "we recommend booking the 1BR for couples" is positioning, not a fact
- **Hedged ranges with explicit caveats** — "₱50–₱150 entrance, confirm at the door" is already self-protecting
- **Standard cultural framing** — "Lipa is heavily Catholic," "lomi is a comfort food" — uncontested generalities
- **General weather patterns** — "rainy season runs June through October" is climate fact, not specific data
- **Direction and geography basics** — "Lipa is south of Manila" doesn't need a citation

If the article's claim is "specific and likely-disputable," it's worth checking. If it's "general and broadly-accepted," skip the verification and put the time into the high-risk claims.

## When a correction is needed (post-publish)

If a published article has an error, the workflow:

1. **Confirm the correction.** Don't change live content based on one Facebook comment unless you've verified it.
2. **Update the article in WordPress.** Edit the post directly. WordPress will bump the "Last modified" date — this is fine and actually helpful for SEO.
3. **Don't change the URL or publish date.** Only the content.
4. **Add a small "Updated YYYY-MM-DD" note** at the top of the article if the correction is substantive (a year, a price, a name). Not needed for tiny copy fixes.
5. **Log the correction** in this guide so we know what to verify next time. Add it under the relevant article's section above.
6. **If the correction came from a commenter,** reply thanking them and confirming the update. This converts a critic into a future advocate.

## How this guide should evolve

This is version 1, written on 2026-05-24 against the June–August 2026 article batch (#15–26 + R1).

Going forward:

- **Add a new article section** to this guide every time a new batch is drafted (so it grows with the blog)
- **Update the holiday calendar table** annually (next: late 2026 for 2027)
- **Revisit the verification source tiers** if any of the named sources (FB pages, official sites) change or go offline
- **Add a "Corrections Log" section** the first time we ship a real correction — track them so we identify patterns
- **If a new content type emerges** (a recipe, a how-to, a partner profile from outreach), add a new "Special Cases" subsection for that type

The point of this guide is not to prevent every error — it's to **make verification a fast, repeatable workflow** so the high-risk errors don't slip through. Voice, opinion, and recommendations don't need this treatment. Dates, RA numbers, religious framing, and operating hours do.
